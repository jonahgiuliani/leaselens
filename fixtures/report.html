Lease review: sample Berkeley apartment lease

High risk

This lease shifts an unusual amount of risk and cost onto you. Two clauses are likely unenforceable in California even if you sign (the oversized deposit and the repair waiver), and several others are worth negotiating before you commit.

Red flags

Likely illegal

Security deposit is three times the legal cap

In your lease: "Tenant shall pay a security deposit equal to three (3) months' rent ($7,200)."

You would hand over $7,200 up front, and money you overpay is hard to claw back.

Cal. Civ. Code §1950.5 (as amended by AB 12, July 1, 2024) caps residential security deposits at one month's rent.

Ask for the deposit to be reduced to one month's rent ($2,400), citing §1950.5.
Likely illegal

You'd waive your right to a habitable home

In your lease: "Tenant accepts the premises 'as is' and waives all claims relating to habitability, and is responsible for all repairs, including plumbing and heating."

Heat, hot water, plumbing and weatherproofing are the landlord's legal job, and you cannot sign that protection away.

Cal. Civ. Code §1941.1 (implied warranty of habitability); waivers are void under §1942.1.

Strike the waiver and restore landlord responsibility for structural, plumbing, heating and safety repairs.
Push back

Late fee is a punitive per-day charge

In your lease: "Rent received after the 3rd incurs a $150 fee plus $25 per additional day."

A late fee must roughly match the landlord's actual costs; compounding daily fees are classic unenforceable penalties.

California courts void late fees that are not a reasonable estimate of damages (unconscionable penalties).

Ask for a single, modest flat fee (around $50) after a 3-5 day grace period.
Push back

Landlord can enter with no notice

In your lease: "Landlord may enter the premises at any time to inspect or show the unit."

You could lose any expectation of privacy in your own home.

Cal. Civ. Code §1954 requires 24-hour written notice, except in emergencies.

Add: entry only on 24-hour written notice, during normal hours, except emergencies.
Push back

Non-refundable "cleaning fee" taken from the deposit

In your lease: "A $400 non-refundable cleaning fee will be deducted from the security deposit."

California does not allow non-refundable deposit deductions; cleaning can only be charged to return the unit to its move-in condition.

Cal. Civ. Code §1950.5(b), (m).

Remove the non-refundable fee; cleaning charges must be itemized against actual condition.
Good to know

Guests cost $50 a night after two nights

In your lease: "Guests staying more than two consecutive nights incur a $50/night charge."

Aggressive guest fees are common friction points with roommates and partners.

No specific cap, but unreasonable guest restrictions can be challenged as interference with quiet enjoyment.

Ask to raise the free allowance to 5-7 nights per month.
Good to know

No subletting, ever

In your lease: "Subletting or assignment of any kind is prohibited."

If you study abroad or leave for a summer internship, you keep paying rent with no options.

California allows reasonable consent standards when the lease requires landlord consent; a total ban is enforceable but inflexible.

Ask for subletting with landlord's consent, not to be unreasonably withheld.

What this lease gets right

Rent is fixed for the full 12-month term. The lease does not require you to waive a jury trial or mandate arbitration.

Email to send your landlord

Subject: Questions about the lease for [unit address] before signing

Hi [landlord name],

Thanks for sending the lease over. I'm excited about the apartment and I'd like to sign soon. Going through it carefully, a few clauses need adjustment before I can do that - two of them conflict with California law:

1. Security deposit. The lease asks for three months' rent ($7,200). Civil Code §1950.5, as amended by AB 12, caps residential deposits at one month's rent. Could you update it to $2,400?

2. Repairs and habitability. The "as is" clause makes the tenant responsible for all repairs, including plumbing and heating, and waives habitability claims. Those responsibilities can't be waived under Civil Code §§1941.1 and 1942.1. Could we restore the standard landlord responsibility for structural, plumbing, heating and safety repairs?

3. Entry. The lease allows entry "at any time." Civil Code §1954 requires 24-hour written notice except in emergencies. Happy to agree to reasonable showings with notice.

4. Late fee. $150 plus $25 per day would likely be treated as an unenforceable penalty. I'd suggest a flat $50 fee after a five-day grace period.

5. Cleaning fee. The $400 non-refundable deduction conflicts with §1950.5, which doesn't permit non-refundable deposit charges. Could we remove it?

Everything else in the lease works for me. If you can send an updated version with these changes, I'll sign the same day.

Thanks,
[your name]
[your phone]

What to do next

Send the email, then wait for the landlord's reply before signing. If they push back on the two illegal clauses, a free tenants' counselor will back you up in one call: Berkeley Rent Stabilization Board, East Bay Community Law Center, or Bay Area Legal Aid. For anything beyond negotiation - a lease you already signed, a deposit not returned - courts.ca.gov self-help walks you through your options.

LeaseLens is not a law firm and this report is not legal advice. It is a plain-English reading of your lease against California tenant law, meant to tell you what to push back on and where to get real help.
